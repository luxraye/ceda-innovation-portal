PinnedScams & feesTebogo Ramasedi · 11 d ago

Nobody can sell you a place in a CEDA call

This portal never asks for a fee to apply, to be scored or to be shortlisted, and evaluators and staff will never ask you for money or gifts. If anyone offers to 'fast-track' your application for a fee, it is a scam. Report it to CEDA and do not pay.

  • Apply only through this portal or official CEDA offices
  • Check that messages about your application also appear in your portal inbox
  • Report suspicious calls or WhatsApp messages to CEDA
  • Paying 'processing' or 'consultant' fees to strangers who promise approval
  • Sharing your login, OTP or Omang copy with anyone who contacts you first
PinnedProtecting your ideaTebogo Ramasedi · 12 d ago

Protect your idea before you share it widely

An idea on its own is hard to protect. What you can protect is your brand, your code and designs, and inventions that are new. In Botswana, patents, trademarks and industrial designs are registered with CIPA. If you plan to patent an invention, file before you publish or pitch it in public, because public disclosure can stop it counting as new.

  • Search CIPA's register before you settle on a business name or logo
  • Keep dated records of your design work (commits, sketches, emails to yourself)
  • Talk to a registered IP agent before a public launch if you have a real invention
  • Posting full technical designs, source code or formulas in forums or on social media
  • Assuming an NDA protects you from everyone: it only binds the people who sign it

Choosing a name? Check it's free first

Before you print anything, check that your business name and logo aren't already registered or in use in your sector. Rebranding after launch is expensive, and a clash can lead to a legal letter.

  • Search CIPA's company and trademark records
  • Check that the domain and social media handles are free too
  • Copying a well-known brand's look 'for inspiration'

Pitch pitfalls

Pitch pitfallsTebogo Ramasedi · 6 d ago

What to share in a pitch, and what to keep back

Panels need to understand how your solution works, not the exact recipe. Explain the problem, the approach and the results. Keep source code, supplier pricing and detailed formulas for due diligence, under an agreement, after you're shortlisted.

  • Describe what your product does and the result it delivers
  • Show a demo or screenshots instead of the code
  • Handing out your full deck with financial models at public events
  • Sharing supplier contracts or pricing in a pitch
Pitch pitfallsTebogo Ramasedi · 8 d ago

Five things that cost marks in a pitch

From panel feedback across past rounds, these are the most common reasons good ideas score lower than they should.

  • State your ask clearly: how much, for what, over how long
  • Show evidence from real customers, even a small pilot
  • Name your competitors and say why you're different
  • Market size from a global report with no Botswana number
  • Reading your slides word for word
  • Saying 'we have no competition'

Contracts & co-founders

Contracts & co-foundersKgosi Mothibi · 7 d ago

Put co-founder agreements in writing early

Most co-founder disputes come from things nobody wrote down: who owns what, who does what, and what happens if someone leaves. A short shareholders' agreement now is far cheaper than a fight later, and CEDA checks youth shareholding at screening.

  • Agree shares, roles and time commitment in writing before you apply
  • Use vesting, so shares are earned over time
  • Make sure the company, not a person, owns the code, brand and domain
  • Splitting shares equally by default without discussing roles
  • Letting a freelancer keep ownership of the code they wrote for you

Scams & fees

Scams & feesTebogo Ramasedi · 1 d ago

Be careful with 'investors' who want fees up front

Real investors make money when you do. Someone who asks for a due-diligence fee, a 'registration' fee or a deposit before investing is very likely running a scam.

  • Ask for references from founders they have funded
  • Have a lawyer read any term sheet before you sign
  • Paying anything to receive an investment

Data & privacy

Data & privacyKgosi Mothibi · 5 d ago

Collecting customer data? The Data Protection Act applies to you

If your product stores names, phone numbers, locations or health information, Botswana's Data Protection Act, 2024 applies. Ask for consent, collect only what you need, and keep it secure. Evaluators look for this in digital ideas.

  • Write a one-page privacy notice in plain language
  • Collect the minimum data you need, and delete it when you're done
  • Use strong passwords and two-factor sign-in on admin accounts
  • Collecting Omang numbers 'just in case'
  • Sharing customer lists with partners without consent

This board is general guidance, not legal advice. For a patent, trademark or contract question, speak to a registered IP agent or a lawyer. Have a question about it? Ask in the forum.
